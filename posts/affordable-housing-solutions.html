Finding housing that fits your budget in Elizabethtown usually comes down to knowing which local office or program handles what. Renting with help, buying your first home with assistance, or building through a nonprofit are all possible here, but each one runs through a different door. This guide walks through the affordable housing options available to Elizabethtown and Hardin County residents, who runs them, and how our team helps buyers use them.

Rental assistance in Elizabethtown and Hardin County

Housing Authority of Elizabethtown

The Housing Authority of Elizabethtown manages public housing in the city. Rent in public housing is based on household income, and eligibility is set by federal HUD rules. Waiting lists are common, so the most useful step is to apply early and keep your contact information current with the office.

Housing Choice Vouchers (Section 8)

A Housing Choice Voucher helps an eligible household rent a home from a private landlord, with the voucher covering part of the rent. In Hardin County, Central Kentucky Community Action Council lists a Section 8 certificate and voucher program and refers families to rental subsidy programs. Central Kentucky Community Action Council serves Hardin County along with LaRue, Nelson, Meade, Breckinridge, Grayson, Marion and Washington counties.

If you live in or near Radcliff

Families closer to Fort Knox can also contact the Housing Authority of Radcliff, which manages public housing in Radcliff. Read our Radcliff page for more on the area.

Income-based apartments

Several apartment communities in Elizabethtown and Radcliff were built under the federal Low-Income Housing Tax Credit program, which means some or all units are set aside for households under an income limit. You apply directly with each property's management office, and each property sets its own application process.

Paths to owning a home on a tighter budget

Kentucky Housing Corporation (KHC) loans and down payment assistance

Kentucky Housing Corporation is the state's housing finance agency. It offers first mortgages (conventional, FHA, VA and USDA) and down payment assistance loans for first-time and repeat buyers. You don't apply to KHC directly. You apply through a KHC participating lender, and income and purchase price limits apply. Ask any lender you talk to whether they originate KHC loans, and compare at least two. Our first-time homebuyer guide for Elizabethtown covers how these programs fit into the buying process.

USDA Rural Development loans

USDA Rural Development offers home purchase loans and home repair loans and grants. Eligibility for a USDA home loan depends on the address, not the town name, so a home a few miles outside Elizabethtown may qualify when one in town does not. Check a specific address on the USDA eligibility map before you fall in love with a house. We check this for every buyer considering a USDA loan.

VA loans for Fort Knox families

Many Elizabethtown buyers are active-duty soldiers, veterans or civilians working at Fort Knox. Eligible service members and veterans can use a VA home loan, which can make buying more affordable than renting. See our Fort Knox housing guide and how BAH fits into your home search. Our team is led by a U.S. Marine Corps veteran who has been through this process.

Hardin County Habitat for Humanity

Hardin County Habitat for Humanity is based in Elizabethtown. Habitat affiliates partner with families who help build their own home and then buy it with an affordable mortgage. The Hardin County affiliate also runs a ReStore in Elizabethtown that sells donated building materials and home goods at reduced prices, which is useful for homeowners working on repairs. Visit hardinhabitat.com for its current programs and application periods.

Tips for finding affordable housing in Elizabethtown

  • Get on waiting lists early. Public housing and voucher programs often have waiting lists, so apply as soon as you think you may qualify.
  • Compare renting and buying honestly. With assistance programs, buying can cost less each month than renting a similar home. Our renting vs. buying guide for Elizabethtown walks through the trade-offs.
  • Look a little outside town. Smaller towns nearby, like Vine Grove, Radcliff and Hodgenville, often offer more house for the money, and some addresses outside city limits qualify for USDA loans.
  • Understand the full monthly cost. Property taxes, insurance and utilities matter as much as the purchase price. Our Elizabethtown property taxes guide explains how taxes work here.
  • Talk to a lender before you shop. A pre-approval tells you which programs you qualify for and what price range makes sense.
