Living in Elizabethtown, KY

Elizabethtown is the county seat of Hardin County and the crossroads of Central Kentucky. I-65 runs along the city's southeast side, the Western Kentucky Parkway begins here and heads west, and the Bluegrass Parkway runs east toward Bardstown and Lexington. That location is a big part of why buyers choose E-town: you get a small-city pace with quick access to Louisville, Fort Knox and the rest of the region.

Getting around

  • Louisville: about 45 minutes north on I-65, or by US-31W (Dixie Highway)
  • Fort Knox: about 15 miles north via the 31W By-Pass and US-31W
  • Bardstown: about 30 minutes east via the Bluegrass Parkway
  • Nashville: about two and a half hours south on I-65

Schools

Two public school districts serve Elizabethtown, and which one a home is zoned for depends on the street address, not just the city name. Elizabethtown Independent Schools includes Elizabethtown High School, T.K. Stone Middle School and Morningside and Helmwood Heights elementary schools. Hardin County Schools serves other parts of the city and the surrounding county, including Central Hardin High School. Private options include Saint James Catholic Regional School and Gloria Dei Lutheran School. For college and career training, Elizabethtown Community & Technical College is in town, and Western Kentucky University operates a regional campus here.

Choosing where to buy in Elizabethtown

Most buyers here don't shop by subdivision name. They narrow things down by a few practical questions, and we help you answer each one for every home you look at:

  • Inside or outside city limits? Homes inside the city pay city property tax and get city services. Homes just outside often come with bigger lots and county services.
  • Which school district? The address decides whether a home is zoned for Elizabethtown Independent Schools or Hardin County Schools.
  • Older or newer? You'll find historic homes near downtown and the Public Square, established neighborhoods across town, and newer construction toward the edges of the city.
  • Which way do you drive? If you commute to Fort Knox or Louisville, living close to US-31W or an I-65 interchange can save time every day.

How the Elizabethtown housing market works

For current prices and days on market, see the Market Trends section on this page. A few things shape this market year after year:

  • Steady demand from Fort Knox. Military and civilian families relocate here every year, and many buy with VA loans, which keeps demand for move-in-ready homes steady.
  • A seasonal rhythm. Spring and early summer bring the most buyers, and summer is peak PCS season at Fort Knox. Late fall and winter are quieter, but homes still sell year-round.
  • Pricing and presentation decide results. Well-priced, well-presented homes move quickly. Overpriced homes or homes that need work tend to sit.
  • New construction takes time. Many new homes are built to order, so buying new usually means planning months ahead rather than moving in right away.

Work, healthcare and everyday life

Fort Knox has long been one of the largest employers for Hardin County residents, which is why so many military and civilian families relocate to Elizabethtown. Baptist Health Hardin, the regional hospital, is in town, with emergency, cardiac, cancer, maternity and pediatric care. Freeman Lake Park, the connected Elizabethtown Nature Park, and a walkable historic downtown round out daily life.
