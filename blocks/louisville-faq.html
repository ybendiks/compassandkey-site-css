Louisville Real Estate FAQ

Is Louisville, KY a good place to live?

For many buyers, yes. Louisville is Kentucky's largest city, where I-64, I-65 and I-71 meet, with an international airport, large employers like UPS, Norton Healthcare, UofL Health, Ford, GE Appliances and Humana, and universities including UofL, Bellarmine and Spalding. It also has Olmsted-designed parks, Waterfront Park and Jefferson Memorial Forest. With very different areas, from Victorian homes in Old Louisville to newer suburban streets, the right fit depends on your commute, schools and budget, and we're happy to talk through it with you.

What is Louisville, KY known for?

Louisville is best known for the Kentucky Derby, run at Churchill Downs on the first Saturday in May at the end of the two-week Kentucky Derby Festival. That's where the nickname Derby City comes from; locals also call it River City, Falls City and The 'Ville. The city is known for its Olmsted parks (Cherokee, Iroquois and Shawnee), Waterfront Park on the Ohio River, Old Louisville's Victorian homes, and the UPS worldwide air hub at Louisville Muhammad Ali International Airport.

How do property taxes work in Louisville and Jefferson County?

The Jefferson County Property Valuation Administrator (PVA) assesses property based on its value as of January 1. The Jefferson County Sheriff's Office collects most property taxes, including state, Louisville Metro, school, fire and other special district taxes, and mails bills in the fall with a discount for paying early. Which taxes apply depends on location: homes within the old City of Louisville boundaries are in the Urban Service District, and many of the county's smaller cities send their own separate tax bills. You can look up any property on the Jefferson County PVA website.

Do you have to pay property tax after age 65 in Louisville?

Yes, but Louisville homeowners 65 and older can lower their tax bill with Kentucky's homestead exemption, which reduces the taxable assessed value of a primary residence. Owners who are totally disabled and live in the home may qualify for a disability exemption instead; only one of the two is allowed per household. Both are handled through the Jefferson County PVA. If you're buying in Louisville as a retiree, we'll remind you to apply once the home is yours.

How do JCPS school assignments work when buying a home in Louisville?

Jefferson County Public Schools uses a managed choice system. Every Louisville address has a resides school, and students are enrolled there by default. Families can also apply to magnet schools and programs, and some addresses fall in a high school Choice Zone, where families apply to choose between a nearby school and one farther away. Applications for the next school year are taken in late fall and processed together, not first come, first served. We check each home's resides schools with the JCPS School Finder before you make an offer.

What help is available for first-time homebuyers in Louisville?

Louisville buyers can use Kentucky Housing Corporation (KHC) programs, including home loans, down payment assistance and homebuyer education, through approved lenders. Louisville Metro also runs its own Down Payment Assistance Program for income-qualified buyers who don't currently own a home. It requires HUD-approved homebuyer counseling, the home must be in Louisville Metro, and applications open for limited windows, so it pays to plan ahead. We can connect you with lenders who use these programs regularly.

Yes, and it's one of the most common moves we handle. Our office is in Elizabethtown, about 45 minutes south of Louisville on I-65, and we work with buyers and sellers on both ends, especially in south Louisville and along the I-65 and Dixie Highway (US-31W) corridors toward Fort Knox, Radcliff and Hardin County. Keep in mind the two areas differ on schools and taxes: Louisville homes are in JCPS and assessed by the Jefferson County PVA, while Hardin County has its own districts and PVA. We'll help you time the sale and purchase so the move works.

When is the best time to sell a house in Louisville?

In Redfin data across many U.S. metros, spring and early summer are the strongest stretch: homes sell fastest around May and prices peak around June, while December through February are the slowest months. Louisville families often want to move before the new JCPS school year, which adds to spring and summer demand. Homes still sell year-round here, and pricing, condition and presentation usually matter more than the calendar. We'll help you choose the right time to list.
