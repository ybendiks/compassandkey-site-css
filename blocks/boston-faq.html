Boston Real Estate FAQ

Is Boston, KY an incorporated city?

No. Boston is an unincorporated community, and the Census Bureau counts it as a census-designated place. It isn't on Kentucky's list of incorporated cities, so it has no city government and no city property tax. Nelson County provides local government. Homeowners in Boston pay the state, county, school and district property taxes on the Nelson County bill, which the Nelson County Sheriff's Office collects. The Nelson County PVA assesses property values and takes homestead exemption applications from owners 65 and older or classified as totally disabled.

Is Boston, KY a good place to live?

It can be, if you want quiet, rural living. Boston is a small Nelson County community on US-62 between Elizabethtown and Bardstown, with its own K-8 school and a long history. The trade-off is distance: groceries, healthcare and most jobs are a drive away in Bardstown or Elizabethtown. Homes here are mostly rural properties, so we help you check septic, water service, flood zone status and USDA eligibility before you make an offer.

What schools serve Boston, KY?

Boston is part of Nelson County Schools. Boston School on Wilson Creek Road serves the early grades through 8th grade, and it feeds Thomas Nelson High School, which has a Bardstown mailing address. Rural addresses near county lines can be assigned differently, so we confirm the school assignment for every home before you write an offer.

How far is Boston, KY from Elizabethtown and Bardstown?

Boston sits on US-62, which runs west to Elizabethtown and I-65 and east about 12 miles to Bardstown. KY 61 (Lebanon Junction Road) heads north from US-62 at Boston toward I-65 and Shepherdsville, a useful route for Louisville commuters. The Bluegrass Parkway is reached from US-62 near Boston. Our office is in Elizabethtown, just down US-62, so Boston is close to home for us.

What is Boston, KY known for?

Bourbon and old history. The Booker Noe Distillery, a Jim Beam bourbon distillery, is in Boston. Mount Moriah Baptist Church, organized in 1802, is one of the community's oldest institutions. The original settlement, Old Boston, sat a little to the east and probably moved in the mid-1800s to be nearer the new railroad and away from flooding. The Boston post office has been open since 1831.
