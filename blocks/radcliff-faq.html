Radcliff Real Estate FAQ

Is Radcliff, KY a good place to live?

For many buyers, especially military families, yes. Radcliff borders Fort Knox, so living here can put you minutes from work on post. Elizabethtown is about 11 miles south and Louisville about 34 miles north on US-31W, with I-65 reachable via the Joe Prather Highway. Hardin County Schools serves the city, and there are city parks like Saunders Springs Nature Preserve. Whether it's the right fit depends on your commute, schools and budget, and we're happy to talk through that with you.

What is Radcliff, KY known for?

Radcliff is best known as the hometown next to Fort Knox. It was laid out in 1919 and named for Major William Radcliffe of the Quartermaster Corps at Camp Henry Knox, and it grew as the post grew. The town is home to Kentucky Veterans Cemetery Central and the Kentucky September 11th Memorial, which includes a beam from the World Trade Center and limestone from the Pentagon. Visitors also come for the Kentucky Museum of American Pocketknives and the trails and waterfall at Saunders Springs Nature Preserve.

What schools serve Radcliff, KY?

Radcliff is part of Hardin County Schools. Most city students attend North Hardin High School, and some addresses are zoned for John Hardin High School. North Middle School and elementary schools such as North Park, Woodland, Meadow View and Radcliff Elementary serve the area, and North Hardin Christian School is a private option. Families who live on Fort Knox are zoned to the post's DoDEA schools instead. Because zoning goes by address, we check each Radcliff home against attendance boundaries before you write an offer.

How do property taxes work in Radcliff and Hardin County?

The Hardin County Property Valuation Administrator (PVA) assesses each property's fair market value as of January 1. The PVA doesn't set rates or collect taxes, and most county bills are collected by the Hardin County Sheriff's Office. Homes inside Radcliff city limits also owe city property tax, which the City of Radcliff's Finance Office bills and collects. Kentucky offers a homestead exemption for homeowners 65 and older and those classified as totally disabled, applied for through the PVA. You can look up any assessment on the Hardin County PVA website.

Do Radcliff sellers accept VA loans?

Yes. With Fort Knox on Radcliff's northern border, VA buyers are a big part of this market and local sellers see VA offers all the time. According to the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, eligible buyers don't need a down payment as long as the price doesn't exceed the appraised value, there's no private mortgage insurance, and the seller may pay closing costs. A one-time VA funding fee applies for most buyers. Our team is led by a Marine Corps veteran, and we write VA offers that Radcliff sellers can trust.

Should I buy in Radcliff or live on post at Fort Knox?

It depends on your timeline, family and plans after this assignment. Living on post keeps you closest to work, and on-post families are zoned to Fort Knox's DoDEA schools. Buying in Radcliff puts you just outside the gate, with Hardin County Schools and the chance to build equity or keep the home as a rental after you PCS. The Fort Knox School Liaison Officer can walk you through school options either way. We help Fort Knox families compare both.

What help is available for first-time homebuyers in Radcliff?

Radcliff buyers can use Kentucky Housing Corporation (KHC) programs, including home loans for first-time and repeat buyers and down payment assistance that's paired with a KHC first mortgage. KHC also keeps a list of homebuyer education agencies across the state, and its loans are offered through participating lenders. Eligible service members and veterans should compare a VA loan too. For homes on the edges of town, check the address on the USDA's eligibility map, since USDA loan eligibility is set by location. We can connect you with local lenders who use these programs every day.

When is the best time to sell a house in Radcliff?

Late spring and early summer are usually the strongest months to sell, and late fall and winter the slowest. Radcliff adds a big local factor: summer is peak PCS season, when many military families move to new duty stations, so a wave of Fort Knox buyers often arrives at the same time. Listing ahead of that window can put your home in front of them. Homes still sell year-round here, and pricing and presentation matter more than the calendar.
