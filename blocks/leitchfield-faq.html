Leitchfield Real Estate FAQ

Is Leitchfield, Kentucky a good place to live?

For buyers who want a small, rural town, it can be. Leitchfield is the county seat of Grayson County, sits right on the Western Kentucky Parkway about 29 miles west of Elizabethtown, and is between two lakes, Rough River and Nolin. It has a regional hospital (Owensboro Health Twin Lakes Medical Center), the Grayson County Schools, an ECTC campus and a historic Court Square. It's quieter than Elizabethtown, with fewer big-box options, so whether it fits depends on your commute and lifestyle. We're happy to talk that through with you.

What is Leitchfield, KY known for?

Leitchfield is known as the "City of Twin Lakes" because Rough River Lake lies to the north and Nolin River Lake to the south. The town was named for Major David Leitch, an aide to George Washington, and was incorporated in 1866. The historic courthouse anchors the Court Square Historic District, where the Twin Lakes National Fiddler Championship is held each year. Locals also turn out for the Grayson County Fair the week before Labor Day and the Ice Cream Festival every July.

What schools serve Leitchfield, KY?

Leitchfield is served by Grayson County Schools. The district includes Grayson County High School, Grayson County Middle School, H.W. Wilkey and Oran P. Lawler elementary schools in and around Leitchfield, elementary schools in Caneyville and Clarkson, and a Technology Center. Leitchfield Christian Academy is a private option, and Elizabethtown Community & Technical College has a Leitchfield campus with associate degrees and continuing education. If a particular school matters to you, we'll confirm assignment with the district for each home before you make an offer.

How do property taxes work in Leitchfield and Grayson County?

The Grayson County Property Valuation Administrator (PVA) assesses each property's value; the PVA doesn't set rates or collect taxes. County property tax bills are collected by the Grayson County Sheriff's Office, and unpaid bills are later turned over to the County Clerk. Homes inside Leitchfield city limits also pay a city property tax set by the city council, so ask City Hall about city bills. Kentucky offers a homestead exemption for homeowners 65 and older and for those classified as totally disabled, which you apply for through the PVA.

Can I buy lake property near Leitchfield?

Yes, many buyers look at homes near Rough River Lake, north of town, or Nolin River Lake, to the south. Both are U.S. Army Corps of Engineers lakes. Rough River Dam State Resort Park has a lodge and marina, and Nolin Lake State Park is nearby. Before you buy, we help you check lake access, dock and shoreline rules, utilities and whether a home sits inside or outside Leitchfield city limits.

What help is available for first-time homebuyers in Leitchfield?

Leitchfield buyers can look at Kentucky Housing Corporation (KHC) programs, which include affordable home loans, down payment assistance and homebuyer education, offered through KHC-approved lenders. Because Leitchfield and much of Grayson County are rural, it's also worth checking a home's address against the USDA eligibility map, since USDA loan eligibility is set by location. We can connect you with local lenders who use these programs regularly.

How far is Leitchfield from Elizabethtown and Louisville?

Leitchfield is about 29 miles west of Elizabethtown on the Western Kentucky Parkway, using Exit 107 (KY-259). From Elizabethtown, the parkway connects to I-65 for Louisville and to US-31W for Fort Knox, so many Leitchfield residents commute east. Heading the other way, the parkway meets I-165 at Beaver Dam for Bowling Green and Owensboro. We often help buyers compare a Leitchfield home with options closer to Elizabethtown so they can weigh price, land and drive time.

When is the best time to sell a house in Leitchfield?

Late spring and early summer are usually the strongest months to sell, and late fall and winter the slowest. Around Leitchfield, warm months also bring lake traffic to Rough River and Nolin, so homes with lake access often draw the most interest then. Homes still sell year-round, and pricing and presentation matter more than the calendar. We'll help you pick the right time to list.
