Hodgenville Real Estate FAQ

Is Hodgenville, KY a good place to live?

For buyers who want small-town, rural living, it can be. Hodgenville is the county seat of LaRue County, about 12 miles from Elizabethtown on KY-61, and LaRue County is part of the Elizabethtown–Fort Knox metro area. One countywide school district has all of its schools in town, and you'll find primary care, a public library and a historic town square close to home. The tradeoff is a drive to Elizabethtown for most jobs, bigger shopping trips and the hospital. We're happy to talk through whether that fits your commute and lifestyle.

What is Hodgenville, KY known for?

Hodgenville is best known as the birthplace of Abraham Lincoln, who was born in 1809 on the Sinking Spring Farm just outside town. Today that site is the Abraham Lincoln Birthplace National Historical Park, where a memorial building holds a symbolic cabin, and the park's Boyhood Home Unit at Knob Creek is northeast of town. Downtown, bronze statues of Lincoln as a boy and as a man stand on the town square near the Lincoln Museum. Every October the town celebrates with Lincoln Days, a two-day festival with a parade and the Railsplitter Run.

What school district serves Hodgenville homes?

All of LaRue County, including Hodgenville, is served by LaRue County Schools. The district's schools are all in Hodgenville: LaRue County High School, LaRue County Middle School, Hodgenville Elementary School, Abraham Lincoln Elementary School and a district preschool. Because every home in the county feeds the same middle and high school, the main thing to check is which elementary school a home is zoned for. We confirm that with the district for any home you're serious about.

How do property taxes work in Hodgenville and LaRue County?

The LaRue County Property Valuation Administrator (PVA) assesses property as of January 1 each year, and the sheriff is responsible for collecting the county, state and school tax bills. Homes inside Hodgenville city limits also pay a city property tax: the city uses the county PVA's assessment, and the City Clerk-Treasurer mails a separate city bill. Kentucky's homestead exemption is available to homeowners 65 and older and to those classified as totally disabled, and you apply through the LaRue County PVA. We'll point out whether a home is inside city limits before you make an offer.

How far is Hodgenville from Elizabethtown, Fort Knox and Louisville?

Elizabethtown is about 12 miles northwest of Hodgenville on KY-61, which also meets I-65 at Elizabethtown's Exit 91. Fort Knox and Louisville are both reached by heading through Elizabethtown, so most commuters use KY-61 every day. US-31E runs through the center of town, about 24 miles northeast to Bardstown and 43 miles south to Glasgow. If you have a daily commute, we can help you look at homes on the side of town that saves you the most time.

What help is available for first-time homebuyers in Hodgenville?

Hodgenville buyers can use Kentucky Housing Corporation (KHC) programs, which include home loans for first-time and repeat buyers, down payment assistance that's paired with a KHC first mortgage, and a statewide list of homebuyer education agencies. These programs are offered through participating lenders. Because LaRue County is largely rural, it's also worth checking homes in and around Hodgenville against the USDA's property eligibility map, since USDA loan eligibility is set by the property's location. We can connect you with local lenders who use these programs.

When is the best time to sell a house in Hodgenville?

Spring and early summer are typically the busiest stretch for home sales, when more buyers are out looking and families want to move before the school year. In Hodgenville, that matters because LaRue County Schools serves the whole county and many buyers plan their move around the school calendar. Homes in Hodgenville still sell year-round, and pricing, condition and presentation matter more than the calendar. We'll help you choose the right time to list and get your home ready.
