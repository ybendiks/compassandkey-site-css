Living in New Haven, KY

New Haven is a small town in southern Nelson County, right by the LaRue County line where US-31E meets KY 52, with the Rolling Fork River along its west side. It began as Pottinger's Landing, founded in 1820 near a flatboat landing and warehouses on the Rolling Fork, where Colonel Samuel Pottinger pictured a shipping point for goods bound for New Orleans. The town was incorporated in 1839 and named for New Haven, Connecticut. Today it's a quiet, rural small town. You won't find big-box shopping here, and most people drive to Bardstown or Elizabethtown for that.

Getting around

  • Bardstown: about 14 miles north on US-31E
  • Hodgenville: about 12 miles south on US-31E
  • Elizabethtown: south through Hodgenville, then KY 61 northwest into Elizabethtown and I-65
  • Louisville: north through Bardstown, where US-31E continues toward Louisville

Schools

New Haven is served by Nelson County Schools. The New Haven School on High Street serves kindergarten through 8th grade, and it feeds Thomas Nelson High School, which sits outside Bardstown's city limits with a Bardstown mailing address. Nelson County homes are assigned to schools by address, so we confirm school assignment for every property before you write an offer.

Choosing where to buy in New Haven

With a town this size, the choice usually comes down to a few practical questions:

  • In town or out in the county? Homes inside city limits pay a City of New Haven property tax and can use city services like sewer and garbage pickup. Homes in the surrounding county often have more land but may rely on a septic system. We confirm which side of the line a home is on and how it's served.
  • Near the river? With the Rolling Fork running past town, we check flood zone status for homes near the river or its creeks before you commit.
  • Which way do you drive? North toward Bardstown and Louisville, or south toward Hodgenville and Elizabethtown. Being near US-31E keeps either drive simple.

Everyday life

New Haven is home to the Kentucky Railway Museum, which moved here in 1990 and runs historic equipment on its own railroad, including the L&N 152 steam locomotive, through the Rolling Fork River valley. The Abbey of Our Lady of Gethsemani, home of the monk Thomas Merton, is nearby. The town has a branch of the Nelson County Public Library, a city police department, volunteer fire service and city recreation. For hospitals, larger stores and most jobs, residents head to Bardstown, Elizabethtown or Louisville.
