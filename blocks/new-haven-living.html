Living in New Haven, KY

New Haven is a small town in southern Nelson County, right by the LaRue County line where US-31E meets KY 52, with the Rolling Fork River along its west side. It began as Pottinger's Landing, founded in 1820 near a flatboat landing and warehouses on the Rolling Fork, where Colonel Samuel Pottinger pictured a shipping point for goods bound for New Orleans. The town was incorporated in 1839 and named for New Haven, Connecticut.

Getting around

  • Bardstown: north on US-31E
  • Hodgenville: about 12 miles south on US-31E
  • Elizabethtown: south through Hodgenville, then KY 61 northwest into Elizabethtown and I-65
  • Louisville: north through Bardstown, where US-31E continues toward Louisville

Schools

New Haven is served by Nelson County Schools. The New Haven School on High Street serves kindergarten through 8th grade. School assignment depends on the home's address, so we check it with the district for every property before you write an offer.

Choosing where to buy in New Haven

A few practical questions help sort homes here:

  • In town or out in the county? Homes inside city limits pay a City of New Haven property tax and can use city services like sewer and garbage pickup. We confirm which side of the line a home is on and how it's served.
  • Which county? New Haven sits right by the LaRue County line, so we confirm the county for each home along with its school assignment.
  • Which way do you drive? North on US-31E toward Bardstown and Louisville, or south toward Hodgenville and Elizabethtown.

Everyday life

New Haven is home to the Kentucky Railway Museum, which moved here in 1990 and runs historic equipment on its own railroad, including the L&N 152 steam locomotive, through the Rolling Fork River valley. The town has a branch of the Nelson County Public Library, a city police department, volunteer fire service and city recreation.
