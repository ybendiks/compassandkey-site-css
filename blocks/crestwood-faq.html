Crestwood Real Estate FAQ

Is Crestwood, KY a good place to live?

For many buyers, yes. Crestwood sits just outside Louisville's Northeast End, about 25 minutes from downtown via I-71 outside rush hour, and it's served by Oldham County Schools, with South Oldham High School right in town. It has its own city government and parks, including The Maples Park and Veterans Memorial Park of Kentucky, plus Yew Dell Botanical Gardens and the South Oldham Library. Whether it fits you depends on your commute, school assignment and budget, and we're happy to talk that through.

Is Crestwood, KY considered part of Louisville?

Not officially. Crestwood is its own home rule-class city in Oldham County, with its own mayor and city commission. It isn't part of Louisville Metro government, which covers Jefferson County. But it sits just outside Louisville's Northeast End, and many residents think of it as a Louisville suburb. For buyers, the difference shows up in practical ways: Oldham County Schools instead of JCPS, the Oldham County PVA and Sheriff for property taxes, and city services if the home is inside Crestwood city limits.

How far is Crestwood from Louisville?

Downtown Louisville is roughly 20 miles from Crestwood, or about 25 minutes via I-71 outside rush hour. Peak traffic can stretch that considerably. KY 329 connects Crestwood to I-71 at Exit 14. KY 22 (Brownsboro Road) and KY 146 (LaGrange Road, through Pewee Valley) both run from Crestwood toward Louisville and meet the Gene Snyder Freeway (I-265), which is often the faster way to East End jobs and shopping. What is Crestwood, KY known for?

Crestwood started as Beard's Station, a railroad stop that grew up in the 1850s after landowner Joseph Beard donated land for the rail line and built the warehouse that served as the station. The name was shortened to "Beard," and the city's history says railroad engineers jokingly called it "Whiskers," which led to the change to Crestwood in 1909. The city incorporated in 1970. Today Crestwood is known for Yew Dell Botanical Gardens, which is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and for The Maples Park, the city's first park.

What schools serve homes in Crestwood, KY?

Crestwood is part of Oldham County Schools. South Oldham High School is in Crestwood, and elementary schools serving the area include Crestwood, Camden Station and Kenwood Station, with South Oldham Middle among the middle schools. Assignments depend on the street address, and some homes with a Crestwood mailing address are zoned for other Oldham County schools. The district publishes school assignment information, and we confirm each home's assigned schools before you make an offer.

How do property taxes work in Crestwood and Oldham County?

The Oldham County Property Valuation Administrator (PVA) assesses each property's value but doesn't set rates or collect taxes. The Oldham County Sheriff's Office collects property tax bills, and the City of Crestwood's own website points residents there for real estate tax information. Whether a home is inside Crestwood city limits can affect its bill, so it's worth confirming with City Hall for a specific address. Kentucky's homestead exemption is available to homeowners 65 and older and to those who are totally disabled, and you apply through the PVA. You can look up any property's assessment on the Oldham County PVA website.

What help is available for first-time homebuyers in Crestwood?

Crestwood buyers can use Kentucky Housing Corporation (KHC) programs. KHC offers home loans and down payment assistance through approved lenders, plus homebuyer education and housing counselors, and its website has an eligibility check and a lender search. Since lender requirements and program rules change, the best first step is a conversation with a KHC-approved lender who works in Oldham County. When is the best time to sell a house in Crestwood?

Across many U.S. metros, spring and early summer are strongest. Homes typically sell fastest in May and close at their highest prices around June, while December through February is the slowest stretch. Crestwood adds a local factor: families shopping by Oldham County school assignment often aim to move before the school year starts, and a spring listing lines up with that. Homes still sell year-round, and pricing and condition usually matter more than the calendar. We'll help you pick the right time to list.
