Living in Louisville, KY

Louisville is Kentucky's largest city and the center of the region our Elizabethtown team serves every day. Since the city and Jefferson County governments merged in 2003, most of the county is governed as Louisville Metro, while a number of smaller incorporated cities inside the county kept their own status. We work with buyers and sellers across Louisville, especially in the south end and along the I-65 corridor, and with families moving between Louisville and Hardin County in either direction.

Getting around

  • Interstates: I-64, I-65 and I-71 meet in Louisville. I-264 (the Watterson and Shawnee Expressways) forms an inner partial loop, and I-265 (the Gene Snyder Freeway) is the outer beltway.
  • Elizabethtown: about 45 minutes south on I-65
  • Fort Knox and Radcliff: south via US-31W (Dixie Highway), which continues on to Elizabethtown
  • Air travel: Louisville Muhammad Ali International Airport, also home to UPS's worldwide air hub

Schools

Jefferson County Public Schools (JCPS) serves Louisville and the rest of Jefferson County. JCPS uses a managed choice system: each address has a "resides" school, and families can also apply to magnet schools and programs, such as duPont Manual, Louisville Male and Central High School Magnet Career Academy. Applications for the next school year are taken in late fall. Higher education includes the University of Louisville, Bellarmine University and Spalding University.

Because the address decides the resides school, we look up each home's assigned schools with you before you write an offer.

Choosing where to buy in Louisville

Louisville is big, so it helps to start broad and narrow down with a few practical questions:

  • Which part of town? Locals talk about the West End, the South End (south and southwest of downtown), and the East End, with the Highlands along Bardstown Road and Old Louisville in between.
  • Older or newer? Old Louisville is known for its Victorian homes, and neighborhoods like Butchertown, Germantown and Portland for shotgun houses. Newer areas built on former farmland tend to have curving streets and cul-de-sacs.
  • Louisville Metro or a small city? Some addresses sit inside one of the county's smaller incorporated cities, which can mean a separate city tax bill and different services. We confirm this for every home.
  • Which way do you drive? If you commute toward Fort Knox, Radcliff or Elizabethtown, living near I-65, Dixie Highway or the Gene Snyder can make the drive easier.

Work, healthcare and everyday life

Large Louisville employers include UPS, Norton Healthcare, UofL Health, Baptist Health, JCPS, Ford (which runs two plants here), GE Appliances and Humana. Frederick Law Olmsted designed Cherokee, Iroquois and Shawnee parks, with Iroquois Park on the city's south side. Jefferson Memorial Forest in southwest Louisville offers miles of trails, and Waterfront Park hosts concerts and festivals on the Ohio River. Every spring the city celebrates the Kentucky Derby Festival, which leads up to the Derby at Churchill Downs on the first Saturday in May.
